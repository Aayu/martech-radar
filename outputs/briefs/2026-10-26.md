## 1. Read first — Nestlé connects product truth to consumer service

**18/20: 5 / 3 / 5 / 5.** FMCG / connected service journey. [Original Salesforce case, 23 October 2026](https://www.salesforce.com/customer-stories/nestle/agentic-consumer-service/). **4 minutes.**

Nestlé's Agentforce implementation answers common consumer questions using shared product and service data. Human escalation preserves conversation history.

**Mechanism:** Consumer question → Service Cloud workflow → Data 360 access to Snowflake product data through a zero-copy connector, plus brand websites and knowledge articles → answer or human handoff with context.

**Outcome / measurement:** Salesforce reports 40% autonomous resolution in pilots and 4.8/5 satisfaction on agent-led interactions. It reports eight weeks to the first launch, but does not disclose sample sizes, pilot dates, response rates or a comparator.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to your customer-data and transformation work: shared information and escalation design shape the service experience.

**Caveat:** Vendor-authored customer case, not independent validation. Resolution and satisfaction do not establish sales lift or answer accuracy, especially for sensitive product questions. The article's global-expansion description does not establish universal deployment.

**Reading question:** What product-data approval and escalation tests would you require before expanding across brands and countries?

**Possible future angle:** Consumer AI needs governed product knowledge and a human handoff that preserves context.

## 2. Read next — Walmart gives each shopping agent a separate identity

**17/20: 5 / 3 / 5 / 4.** Large-enterprise customer technology. [Original Walmart announcement, 21 October 2026](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/10/Making-It-Possible-for-Agents-to-Shop-on-Behalf-of-Customers). **5 minutes.**

Walmart describes Agent Profiles, an Agent Gateway and operating terms for external shopping agents. It separates delegated purchases from purchases made directly by the customer.

**Mechanism:** Agent starts a task → customer links a dedicated profile through Walmart sign-in → purchase approvals or spending limits → separately visible purchases and revocable access. The gateway is intended to offer a structured connection rather than browser navigation.

**Status / outcome:** Profiles are planned for the following month. The [opened Gateway page](https://www.walmart.com/cp/walmart-agent-gateway/1280482) explicitly says invitation-only early beta, with features subject to change. No adoption, reliability or conversion result is disclosed.

**Why it matters:** This makes consent, identity and transaction controls concrete for commerce platform leadership. It is separate from the draft Poppy protocol already in the archive.

**Caveat:** A design and rollout announcement, not proof of production effectiveness. Company agent terms are not legislation. No purchase or sign-in flow was tested.

**Reading question:** Which actions require fresh approval even when an agent has spending permission?

**Possible future angle:** Delegated shopping needs its own identity, permission and activity record.
