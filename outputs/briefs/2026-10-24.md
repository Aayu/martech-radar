## 1. Read first — PepsiCo connects shopper signals to store action

**16/20: 5 / 3 / 3 / 5.** FMCG / commerce operating model. [Original PepsiCo article, 21 October 2026](https://www.pepsico.com/en/newsroom/stories/2026/helping-retailers-shape-future-shopping). **4 minutes.**

PepsiCo explains Perfect Store through specific tools and frontline workflows. It connects outlet understanding with assortment, shelf execution and retailer actions.

**Mechanism:** Store DNA combines sales, local points of interest, demographics and foot traffic; sales-force automation turns information into field tasks. PepsiConnect supports retailer communications, promotions and orders; Pep IRiS turns shelf photos into insights about gaps or misplaced products. Human teams interpret recommendations for the retail setting.

**Why it matters:** This maps to your target commerce and transformation roles: specify the decision, the person responsible and the action after a signal arrives.

**Outcome / measurement:** The company describes a Protein Bay example, but gives no quantified sales lift, adoption denominator or controlled evaluation.

**Caveat:** A new explanatory article about an existing ecosystem; it does not establish that the named tools launched this week. Company claims, without independent validation.

**Reading question:** Which signal should change assortment, which should create a field task, and who verifies completion?

**Possible future angle:** A useful customer-data system closes the loop between insight, decision and execution.

## 2. Read for measurement discipline — Tecovas' Wallet-to-credit pilot

**16/20: 5 / 3 / 3 / 5.** Connected journey / loyalty. [Original Angle case](https://www.anglehq.com/case-studies/tecovas-wallet-pilot). **Pilot observation:** 1–21 September 2026, Central time. **Publication date:** not displayed; selected on its explicit recent pilot dates. **4 minutes.**

Tecovas linked enrollment and Wallet activation to promotional credit. Angle reports participant purchase behavior rather than claiming a measured sales lift.

**Mechanism:** Hospitality beyond a visit → enrollment → pass-download event connected to credit → Wallet recognition and Shopify credit at POS → qualifying purchases and repeat buying.

**Outcome / measurement:** 34.7% of participant orders used credit; 14% of purchasing participants bought at least twice. The window includes post-enrollment paid or partially refunded orders, excluding tests, cancellations and fully refunded orders. Credit includes all sources; enrollment dates vary.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to your lifecycle and CRM work: implementation use and incremental business value require different measurement designs.

**Caveat:** Vendor-supplied observations without a control group or sample counts. Different enrollment dates create unequal time to repeat; credit use does not isolate the pilot's promotional credit.

**Reading question:** How would a randomized invitation holdout change the interpretation?

**Possible future angle:** First prove customers use the loyalty journey, then test whether it changes behavior.
