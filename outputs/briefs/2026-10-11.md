## 1. Read first — Shopify's agent-assisted checkout

**Score:** 18/20 · **Source date:** 28 September 2026 · **Read:** 3 minutes · [Original Shopify announcement](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/webmcp-support-for-checkout)

Shopify says browser agents can now read and update checkout state and submit an order after buyer confirmation. The buyer takes control when required, including authentication. This extends agent interaction from discovery and cart management into checkout.

**Why it matters to you:** Your work spans customer journeys, identity, consent and commerce. The useful question is how an agent-mediated purchase changes attribution, customer-data capture and the handoff to a human.

**Read with this question:** Which parts of the journey should an agent be allowed to complete, and what needs an explicit consumer decision?

**Caveat:** This is a platform capability announcement, not evidence of merchant adoption or conversion uplift. The attribution implication is an inference to investigate.

**Possible future angle:** “Agentic commerce is becoming an operating-design question: where should automation stop and consumer control begin?”

## 2. Read for the method — Klover's continuous CRM testing

**Score:** 16/20 · **Source:** vendor case study, date not shown on the opened page · **Read:** 4 minutes · [Original Braze case study](https://www.braze.com/customers/klover)

Klover moved from a weekly, manual creative test to continuously testing message-element combinations in push notifications. Braze reports a 17% overall lift in push opens and top-variant lifts of 22.7% and 47.3% against long-standing controls.

**Why it matters to you:** This maps directly to lifecycle experimentation at Samsung scale. The transferable idea is to test the decision system and allocation cadence, not just another line of copy.

**Read with this question:** If your existing CRM controls stopped improving, would more creative variants help, or would you redesign the experiment and measurement approach?

**Caveat:** The figures come from Braze and its customer. Open rate is an engagement measure; the page mentions revenue contribution but does not establish a generalizable incremental revenue lift. Publication date was not visible, so treat this as a useful case study rather than breaking news.

**Possible future angle:** “When CRM performance plateaus, change the testing system before blaming the message.”

## 3. Skim — Shop Campaigns enters sales analysis

**Score:** 14/20 · **Source date:** 1 October 2026 · **Read:** 2 minutes · [Original Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/new-shop-campaigns-metrics-and-dimensions-on-api-version-2026-10)

ShopifyQL's 2026-10 version adds Shop Campaign orders and dimensions that let teams break down sales by campaign. The change enables a closer link between a commerce campaign and orders within Shopify's reporting environment.

**Why it matters to you:** It is a compact example of the measurement layer catching up with activation. It also suggests a broader explainer on what campaign-level sales reporting can and cannot prove.

**Read with this question:** Does a campaign-associated order show causality, or only a reporting relationship? What holdout or incrementality test would you need?

**Caveat:** The documentation describes fields, not an effectiveness study. The measurement question is analytical inference, not a Shopify claim.

**Possible future angle:** “Better campaign reporting is useful, but attribution and incrementality are different questions.”
