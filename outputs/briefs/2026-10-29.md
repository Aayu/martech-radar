## 1. Read first — Unilever puts cultural analysis inside design teams

**16/20: 5 / 3 / 4 / 4.** FMCG operating model. [Original Unilever account](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2026/how-design-is-driving-desire-across-unilevers-personal-care-portfolio/). **Date:** global newsroom listing says 23 October 2026; regional listing says 28 October. Article body does not display a publication date. **4 minutes.**

Unilever describes AI-assisted semiotics feeding brand-specific design decisions. Designers now work inside eight Personal Care Power Brand teams.

**Mechanism:** Social and cultural signals → agency AI analysis through a brand lens → human semioticians interpret → embedded designers apply insights to packaging and communications. Pepsodent provides a concrete example of simplifying packs and amplifying its smile identity.

**Outcome / measurement:** The LUX example reports over 70% of new consumers recruited through e-commerce and social commerce, without a disclosed period or denominator. This describes channel contribution, not AI-caused acquisition or incremental growth.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to your transformation lens: connect analysis to a team with responsibility for changing the consumer experience.

**Caveat:** Company interview; no controlled design test or validation of the AI analysis. “Data removes subjectivity” is the interviewee's assertion, not an established property of the method.

**Reading question:** How would you test whether culturally informed design performs better than the existing approach?

**Possible future angle:** AI insight becomes useful when someone can turn it into a specific design decision.

## 2. Read for the journey — Terry's turns a gifting tension into a purchase mechanic

**15/20: 5 / 2 / 3 / 5.** Connected campaign. **Evergreen exception:** unusually explicit purchase-entry and retailer adaptation mechanics; campaign year unverified. [Original Jellybean case](https://www.jellybeancreative.co.uk/portfolio/terrys-shopper-campaign-will-you-gift-it-or-give-in/). Archive lists the case on 23 October 2026; campaign described as 14 weeks around Father's Day and teacher gifts. **4 minutes.**

Terry's used the tension between gifting a Chocolate Orange and eating it yourself. The promotion connected the pack to immediate digital rewards.

**Mechanism:** Store displays and screens → qualifying pack purchase → QR scan → barcode/batch-code entry and a gifting confession → instant eVoucher result plus prize draw. Tesco used a separate SMS route; receipt upload was unnecessary.

**Outcome / measurement:** Agency reports over 54,000 entries, 69% eVoucher redemption and a seven-point awareness increase. It does not disclose comparison design, unique buyers or the amount behind its sales-uplift claim.

**Why it matters:** A practical case for your commerce roles: retailer requirements and participation friction shape the journey.

**Caveat:** Agency claims, not causal proof. Pack-code entry does not establish unique purchase verification; no CRM opt-in or retention flow is demonstrated.

**Reading question:** Would removing receipt upload improve participation enough to offset weaker purchase validation?

**Possible future angle:** Connected campaigns need a clear consumer action and a practical route back to value.
