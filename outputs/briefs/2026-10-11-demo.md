## 1. Read first — P&G moves from past purchasers to predicted future buyers

**Beat:** FMCG / CPG. **Score:** 17/20. **Source:** [Sam’s Club Connect, 17 September 2026](https://corporate.walmart.com/about/samsclub/news/2026/09/turning-membership-into-momentum). **Time:** 3 minutes.

P&G wanted new buyers for Cascade Platinum Plus. Sam’s Club says its model used membership and shopping signals to select 800,000 households likely to buy, then measured actual purchases against predicted 12-month value. It reports 2.3× “marketing effectiveness” and 94% accuracy in the 12-month purchase prediction.

**Why it matters to your work:** This is a concrete example for the audience strategy, retail media and measurement responsibilities in the Reckitt, PepsiCo and McKinsey-style roles. The operating idea is to use first-party retailer signals to identify *future* customers, then carry the prediction into a longer measurement window.

**Mechanism:** Membership purchase and intent signals → predictive prospect and lifetime-value models → advertiser campaign to likely new buyers → actual purchase and predicted-value comparison. Sam’s Club also describes an expanded measurement view across media, in-club demos, direct mail and Scan & Go ads.

**Evidence caution:** These are Sam’s Club’s claims. “Marketing effectiveness” is not defined clearly enough in the article to treat 2.3× as incremental sales lift; the 94% figure describes prediction accuracy, not campaign causality.

**Question while reading:** What holdout or matched comparison would show that the predictive audience generated more *incremental* buyers than a simpler category audience?

**Possible later angle:** Better targeting models matter only when the business can prove new customer growth over a sensible time horizon.

## 2. Read next — an owned-data campaign with a connected journey

**Beat:** Campaign / customer journey. **Score:** 16/20. **Source:** [Søstrene Grene and IMPACT case study](https://impactcommerce.com/cases/award-winning-first-party-data-strategy-for-sostrene-grene/). **Date:** The case page does not state a publication date; it reports Q4 campaign results and a database position at the beginning of 2026. **Time:** 6 minutes. **Label:** Evergreen case, not fresh news.

The retailer used a real consumer tension: popular Christmas products sell out. It offered early access in return for an opt-in, then collected leads from a live shopping event, site pop-ups and cards in 30,000 delivered orders. More than 200 creative variations and weekly CRM journeys continued the relationship through Q4.

**Why it matters to your work:** This shows how a campaign can build an owned audience and use that audience across acquisition, commerce and lifecycle stages. It is more useful than an isolated ad example because the data capture and follow-up journey are visible.

**Mechanism:** Fear of missing seasonal products → early-access value exchange → online and parcel touchpoints → lead capture → optimized paid creative and CRM → purchase. The agency says the work added 447,327 leads, 20% of new leads bought in Q4, and CRM activation was associated with 128,071 orders and more than DKK 55 million in revenue.

**Evidence caution:** The figures come from the agency case study. It does not disclose a control group or the calculation behind its reported 37× return on marketing investment, so the attributed orders should not be described as incremental sales.

**Question while reading:** Which piece of the journey created durable customer value: the early-access offer, the lead capture points, or the follow-up CRM?

**Possible later angle:** The best connected campaigns offer a consumer benefit that makes first-party data collection feel natural.

## 3. Skim — Walmart links supplier insight to action

**Beat:** Fortune 500 enterprise technology. **Score:** 15/20. **Source:** [Walmart Data Ventures, 16 September 2026](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/09/walmart-data-ventures-commerce-intelligence). **Time:** 3 minutes.

Walmart describes Scintilla as an integrated view of shopper and business signals for suppliers. Its existing Insights Activation connects those insights to Walmart Connect media; AI summaries and alerts are meant to speed diagnosis, while wider inventory and in-store workflows are part of the continuing roadmap.

**Why it matters to your work:** This is an enterprise platform pattern: data becomes valuable when a merchant or brand team can move from a detected problem to a media, shelf or replenishment decision. It is relevant to commerce platform strategy and cross-functional operating design.

**Mechanism:** First-party retail data → integrated boards and AI interpretation → campaign activation now, with broader replenishment and store action envisaged. Walmart gives the example of spotting an item at risk of running out in 50 stores and routing a user to replenishment.

**Evidence caution:** The article is mainly a product vision. It does not provide adoption, conversion or sales results for the newly described capabilities; several features are explicitly planned for the future.

**Question while reading:** What data ownership, decision rights and workflow integrations would make this insight-to-action loop reliable at scale?

**Possible later angle:** The real retail media platform advantage may be the connection between customer demand, media spend and physical availability.

## Why this is a useful pilot

The three reads connect **prediction → activation → commerce outcome**, but their evidence differs. The P&G example has a quantified model claim; the Søstrene Grene example exposes the full customer journey but reports attributed outcomes; Walmart lays out an operating architecture with limited outcome evidence. That distinction is exactly what the daily radar should preserve.
