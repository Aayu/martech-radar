## Read first — Unilever's demand-to-supply tech loop

**Score:** 18/20 (role relevance 5, evidence 4, novelty 4, practical learning 5). **Beat:** major FMCG / CPG. **Original source and date:** [Unilever, 13 August 2026](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2026/building-resilience-driving-growth-inside-unilevers-aipowered-supply-chain/). **Read:** about 7 minutes.

Unilever describes an AI-enabled supply chain operating model that links demand creation with fulfilment. The useful part is not the generic "AI in supply chain" claim; it is the loop from social listening and live consumer demand to retailer forecast data, material sourcing, production, logistics and on-shelf availability.

**Why it matters to your work:** This is the kind of operating story senior MarTech, commerce and consumer digital roles need: consumer signals do not create business value until they change planning, content, retail execution or availability. It is a good counterweight to thin campaign stories because it shows the plumbing behind a launch.

**Mechanism:** Social listening shaped the Dove x Crumbl launch at Walmart. Unilever says its AI-enabled customer connectivity model with Walmart integrates forecast and sales data, connecting consumer purchases back to material sourcing. Its Forecast Engine Utility produces a 104-week forecast weekly across more than 5 million product-customer combinations in 40 markets. The World Cup example adds a scale test: 35 brands, 180 limited-edition products, over 120 countries, millions of retail locations and more than 50,000 creators.

**Outcome and measurement:** Unilever says the Mexico pilot delivered 98%+ on-shelf availability, reduced inventory and supported category growth. It also reports 31,000 daily factory-to-consumer orders in Hefei, 75% faster fulfilment and logistics costs down almost a quarter. For operations, it reports 8% higher OEE and 20% waste reduction at Hefei, and 20% waste reduction plus 10% capacity uplift at Raeford.

**Read with this question:** What consumer signals should a CPG company connect all the way into supply, rather than leaving them inside a campaign dashboard?

**Evidence caveat:** This is a company-authored interview. It gives mechanisms and operating metrics, but it does not separate incremental growth from baseline growth, and it does not provide retailer-side validation.

**Possible later angle:** The best consumer digital teams connect demand sensing to fulfilment; otherwise personalization just creates better-looking demand that the business may not be ready to serve.

## Read second — a football pitch as a commerce interface

**Score:** 16/20 (role relevance 4, evidence 3, novelty 5, practical learning 4). **Beat:** connected campaign / customer journey, with large marketplace relevance. **Original source and date:** [Ads of the World case study, June 2026](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/field-barcode-case-study); additional result detail reviewed from [Unblock Coffee's campaign summary](https://www.unblock.coffee/campaign/field-barcode/). **Read:** about 5 minutes.

Mercado Livre and GUT São Paulo turned the football pitch into a scannable barcode, readable from live broadcasts, TV screens, photos and social posts. A fan's attention during the match became a commerce action instead of a passive sponsorship impression.

**Why it matters to your work:** It is a useful connected-journey case because the touchpoints are easy to reconstruct: physical venue, broadcast, social sharing, mobile scan, shopping destination and measurable response. It also teaches how commerce can be embedded in culture without forcing people into a separate campaign microsite first.

**Mechanism:** Insight: football attention in Brazil is concentrated but stadium sponsorships are easy to ignore. Trigger: a giant barcode created through grass patterns on the pitch. Decisioning: no complex personalization; the interface is contextual and universal. Touchpoints: stadium, TV broadcast, photos, social posts and mobile scan. Action: viewers scan and enter Mercado Livre. Measurement: successful scans, platform sessions and reported commerce outcomes.

**Outcome and measurement:** Ads of the World reports thousands of successful scans and a 7% increase in Mercado Livre sessions during the campaign period. Unblock Coffee reports R$8.9 million in merchandise GMV, 53,000 coupon redemptions and a 7% week-over-week session lift worth R$8 million in incremental revenue.

**Read with this question:** When is a media placement only attention, and when can it become a functional interface?

**Evidence caveat:** The main case source is campaign/agency supplied. The metrics are useful, but they should be read as attributed campaign results, not independently audited incrementality.

**Possible later angle:** Connected commerce does not always need more channels; sometimes it needs one physical surface that can become a measurable action.
