## 4. Flipkart — the deepest practical read

Flipkart researchers describe a production workflow that selects high-intent mobile-phone searches with no click, researches relevant products using external and catalog data, validates details, and sends personalized recommendations through WhatsApp. In a 23-day deployment, about 15,000 messages generated 37,258 tracked visits; the paper reports roughly 285% higher click-through rate than historical WhatsApp campaign baselines.

**Why read it:** It joins search, catalog, AI quality checks and CRM in one actual customer journey, with India-specific execution detail. **Caution:** The comparison is historical, not a randomized holdout; forwarded messages may inflate tracked clicks, and the paper does not prove incremental purchases. **Reading question:** What consent and frequency controls would you require before scaling this journey?

## 5. Unilever — a creator campaign with a commerce path

House of Fresh gave creators filmable experiences around the FIFA World Cup and used Amazon partner creators to make some content shoppable. Unilever reports 2,100+ creators, 6,000 content pieces and 2.5 billion reach; it says the creator commerce content achieved 5× the reach of traditional brand advertising.

**Why read it:** A large FMCG brand designed an experience as a content system, then linked some content to a product path. **Caution:** Reach and engagement are disclosed, but the article gives no sales lift or conversion figure for the Amazon handoff. **Reading question:** Which link would let the team measure whether a creator's content caused a purchase?

## 6. Kellanova — clean-room segmentation tied to category behavior

Kellanova and LiveRamp combined market and addressable data to distinguish price-conscious Special K lapsed buyers from loyal buyers, then used different creative across Meta and YouTube. LiveRamp reports a 9% sales lift in the price-sensitive group and a 36% sales increase among loyal buyers.

**Why read it:** It closely matches your customer-data and clean-room portfolio and shows that audience design can start from a business problem, not demographics. **Caution:** The vendor case gives no publication date or clear holdout methodology; treat its lift figures as reported, not independently established causality. **Reading question:** How would you set up mutually exclusive audiences and a control group to validate both lifts?

## 7. Shopify — an early signal on agent-readable commerce

Shopify says Stanley 1913 recorded five times as many AI-referred US orders year over year, while ILIA's AI-referred sales nearly tripled. The practical idea is that product data, brand explanations and trusted mentions outside the site need to be legible to AI shopping tools.

**Why read it:** This changes who owns product discovery: commerce, content, PR and data teams all have a role. **Caution:** These are Shopify and merchant examples, with no starting volumes or controlled comparison. Survey intent to use AI should not be described as actual adoption. **Reading question:** What would a brand need to measure to separate new demand from traffic that simply moved from search to AI referral?

## 8. Adobe — watch the move from rules to next-best action

Adobe describes Marketo Optimizer as reasoning across person, account and buying-group signals to choose next actions across marketing, paid media and business development; Marketo Qualifier joins prospecting and lead qualification. This is a B2B example, so its direct fit with your consumer roles is lower, but the architecture is useful.

**Caution:** It is a vendor product announcement with a hypothetical journey, not a measured customer implementation. **Reading question:** Which decisions should remain deterministic, and which could safely be optimized from changing customer signals?
