## 1. Amazon ad auctions: read the regulator’s allegations alongside the company’s response

**18/20:** role relevance 5 · evidence quality 4 · novelty 4 · practical value 5. **Read: 7 minutes**, starting with the release and response, then the complaint introduction. Evidence score reflects primary documentation, not proof that the allegations are true.

**Original development:** [FTC update, 5 November 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/11/new-details-emerge-amazon-ads-auction-rigging-case). This is a material disclosure update to the August lawsuit, not a new lawsuit or effective regulation. The FTC says newly unredacted details describe undisclosed advertising-price surcharges and misleading explanations to advertisers.

**Mechanism and evidence:** the dispute concerns whether reserve pricing raised charges above the auction outcomes advertisers were led to expect. The [case timeline](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/amazon) lists the November update; the linked [181-page complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Amazon-RedactedComplaint.pdf) bears an October 28 filing stamp and retains redactions. This brief therefore avoids reproducing the release’s headline numerical allegations as established losses.

**Counterpoint and outcome:** [Amazon’s response to the original lawsuit](https://www.aboutamazon.com/company-news/amazon-ftc-sponsored-ads-lawsuit-response) rejects deception and advertiser harm, and argues that relevance-based auctions improve advertiser results. Those are company claims, not adjudicated findings. No judgment or independently verified causal loss is established by these sources.

**Why it matters for you:** retail-media governance sits alongside attribution and ROAS. My practical inference: retain bid, charge, placement, conversion and pricing-rule records before attributing a performance change to any single cause.

**Reading question:** what evidence would distinguish auction-rule changes from competition, conversion changes and measurement errors?

**Possible future angle:** “A retail-media dashboard needs an explanation of price, as well as a report of performance.” Requires your own examples before writing.

## 2. Amazon moves shopping into an existing text conversation

**17/20:** role relevance 5 · evidence quality 3 · novelty 4 · practical value 5. **Read: 5 minutes.** Beats: enterprise and connected journey.

**Source:** [Amazon’s text-to-shop announcement](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-text-to-shop). The primary body says “starting today” without a displayed date; [Reuters reporting dated 6 November](https://www.aol.com/articles/amazon-rolls-text-based-shopping-140147000.html) corroborates launch timing.

Amazon is inviting a small US customer subset to shop with Alexa by text. This adds a consumer interaction channel, distinct from the merchant-feed onboarding covered on 5 November.

**Mechanism:** invitation → terms and phone linking → text/photo request → recommendations → cart and purchase. The detergent-reorder example explicitly requires approval; preferences and shopping history support personalization. Cross-channel continuity across text, app, web and devices is rolling out; combining order notifications and deals in that thread is described as a future development.

**Outcome and caveat:** no text-specific adoption, conversion lift or experiment is disclosed. Amazon’s broader Alexa usage figures cannot establish results for this new channel. Access is invitation-only; signing up for news does not guarantee immediate access.

**Why it matters for you:** this is a lifecycle and identity problem as much as a shopping interface. My inference: judge it on account continuity, purchase permissions, channel consent and recovery when an action goes wrong.

**Reading question:** where should the system require a fresh approval rather than relying on a remembered preference?

**Possible future angle:** “The difficult part of conversational commerce is deciding what a conversation authorizes.”
