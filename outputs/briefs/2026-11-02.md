## 1. Read first — American Express makes purchase intent explicit

**17/20: 5 / 3 / 4 / 5.** Enterprise commerce architecture / consent. [Original American Express technical account, 13 October 2026](https://americanexpress.io/intent-intelligence-building-trust-in-agentic-commerce/). **5 minutes.**

Amex describes capturing and authenticating a shopper's instructions before an agent purchases. Its design carries those constraints into payment-credential scoping and subsequent authorization checks.

**Mechanism:** User confirms constraints and payment choice → issuer authentication/sign-off → Intent ID and Proof of Intent Token → scoped payment credential. Constraints include budget, expiry and conditional purchases; lifecycle services can update or cancel intent.

**Why it matters:** Connects your identity and consent work to transaction execution. The transferable question is how a customer's permission survives a handoff between agent, wallet and issuer.

**Outcome / measurement:** No adoption, dispute reduction or conversion result is disclosed.

**Caveat:** Company architecture account, not proof of general availability, interoperability or enforcement. A token does not demonstrate that every constraint is checked correctly. Implementation and use-case requirements vary.

**Reading question:** What must happen if the cart changes after the customer signs off?

**Possible future angle:** Delegated purchasing needs permission that can be checked at execution.

**Repeat distinction:** The archive's Poppy and Walmart items address agent identity and permissions. This account adds issuer sign-off, an intent record and scoped payment credentials; it is not an update to those separate projects.

## 2. Read for the operating lesson — HMRC coordinates search across campaigns

**16/20: 4 / 3 / 4 / 5.** Secondary campaign operations / measurement. [Original Government Communications case, 20 October 2026](https://www.communications.gov.uk/case-study/hm-revenue-and-customs-creates-connected-search-to-streamline-campaigns/). **3 minutes.** Campaign and comparison dates are not disclosed.

HMRC replaced separate campaign search plans with shared planning and governance. It brought paid search, SEO, content and digital teams into a common operating model.

**Mechanism:** Customer search demand → shared keyword decisions and pooled budgets → always-on coverage, intensified around campaign moments → coordinated content and search activity.

**Outcome / measurement:** The government case reports up to 75% CPC improvement across campaigns and 500,000 Google AI Overview impressions for Tax Confident. No baseline period, spending mix, control group or downstream service outcome is supplied; these are reported efficiency and visibility measures.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to multi-brand transformation: shared governance may expose competing budgets and duplicated work before another tool is purchased.

**Caveat:** Self-reported case. Lower CPC does not establish better-qualified visits, completed customer tasks or incremental impact. Planned expansion into additional AI bidding and search products is not treated as deployed.

**Reading question:** Would consolidated bidding improve completed customer tasks at the same total cost?

**Possible future angle:** Shared search governance can be an operating-model decision with a measurable test.
