# Martech Radar — first-run editorial audit

Date: 15 November 2026

## Editorial purpose

Martech Radar is not a newsboard. It is a research system for building an informed executive point of view on consumer digital, marketing technology, advertising technology and digital commerce.

A useful signal should help answer at least three of these questions:

1. What changed?
2. How does it work in practice?
3. What evidence or outcome exists?
4. What should a marketing, digital or commerce leader infer from it?
5. What is the important caveat?

The collector may retain broad discovery coverage, but only a small number of items should reach the daily leadership shortlist.

## What the first run produced

- 14 sources checked.
- 558 new links discovered.
- 24 articles extracted and placed in Candidates.
- 54 articles left in the extraction queue.
- 678 records in the Library after combining the run with the migrated history.
- Six sources supplied all 24 extracted candidates.
- 22 of 24 candidates were tagged as AI implementation.
- 17 were tagged commerce and measurement.
- 16 were tagged connected journey.
- Six were tagged lifecycle and loyalty.
- Two were tagged customer data.
- None of the 24 represented regulation.

This is too concentrated. The run proved that collection works, but the old ranking rewarded repeated keywords more than leadership value.

## Signals worth keeping

### 1. Opella and Microsoft’s content supply chain

This is the best first-run example. It describes a real operating model, not merely a product announcement: more than 21,000 assets, thirtyfold output growth, engagement reported at 50%, 85 people across six hubs, and a verification layer using 14 specialist agents. It connects technology, workflow redesign, governance and measurable scale. The reported outcomes are company claims and still need independent or causal qualification.

Original: [Consumer Goods Technology](https://consumergoods.com/how-opella-microsoft-use-agentic-ai-build-enterprise-content-supply-chain)

### 2. IAB’s AI-attribution framework

This is useful because it changes the measurement vocabulary. It distinguishes human-owned, AI-assisted and AI-executed conversion paths and separates eligibility to claim influence from actual attribution credit. It is not an outcome story, but it is a consequential standards and measurement story.

Original: [MarTech](https://martech.org/iab-unveils-framework-for-measuring-ais-part-in-conversions/)

### 3. Walmart and Amazon’s agentic-commerce infrastructure

These items are strategically useful because they show how product data, discovery and retail-media infrastructure may change when shopping agents mediate the journey. They need deeper reading for mechanics and implications; they should not be treated as proven performance cases.

Originals: [Walmart infrastructure](https://consumergoods.com/walmart-builds-infrastructure-agentic-commerce-implications-brands), [Amazon product data](https://brandequity.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/digital/amazons-plan-to-own-the-worlds-product-data-and-fight-ai-shopping-agents/135827026)

### 4. Negative evidence and governance failures

The stories about AI-derived audience insight hurting performance and Meta inserting unapproved discounts are more valuable than routine product hype. Failures reveal control, measurement and operating-model weaknesses. They require verification of the underlying study or incident before publication.

### 5. Connected campaign mechanics

Fetch’s rewarded microseries is worth retaining because it links content viewing, rewards and shopping intent. It is a mechanism story, not yet a results story. It belongs on a watchlist until campaign outcomes are reported.

## Items that should not reach the leadership shortlist

- Generic summit recaps, predictions and executive debates without a real implementation or new evidence.
- Basic explainers such as whether a company should have a loyalty program.
- Partnership announcements that describe intent but no implemented workflow, feature or outcome.
- Creative campaign coverage with no meaningful technology or connected-journey mechanism. The Netflix 35mm story is the clearest first-run false positive.
- Broad retail or company-performance news where technology is incidental.
- Repeated versions of the same company story, such as multiple overlapping Wayfair AI articles.
- Old vendor case studies promoted as current news. They may remain useful in the Library, but freshness must affect the daily shortlist.
- Stories matched through navigation, footer or related-article text rather than the article’s actual subject.

## Missing or underrepresented beats

1. **Adtech and media infrastructure** — ad buying, ad serving, programmatic supply, retail media, CTV, conversion APIs, clean rooms, identity and privacy-preserving measurement.
2. **Major platform changes** — Adobe, Meta, Google Ads, Amazon Ads, TikTok, Salesforce and The Trade Desk, with a distinction between a launch and an implemented result.
3. **Measurement and economics** — incrementality, holdouts, MMM, attribution limits, cost, ROAS and business impact.
4. **Customer data and martech architecture** — CDPs, identity, consent, orchestration, decisioning, DAM, content supply chains and operating-model redesign.
5. **Campaigns with connected journeys and outcomes** — not campaigns that are merely creative or popular.
6. **Failures and counter-evidence** — product failures, measurement failures, governance incidents and examples where automation reduced performance.
7. **Regulation and standards** — effective rules, enforcement, technical standards and implementation duties, separated from proposals and consultation drafts.
8. **Non-AI innovation** — AI should be a technology layer, not the organizing subject of almost every item.
9. **India and APAC implementation** — especially consumer-data, retail-media, commerce and consent developments with practical implications.

## Refined daily selection contract

The dashboard now starts with a maximum of five **Leadership Signals**. A story enters only when it has one of these evidence patterns:

- a quantified or business outcome;
- failure or counter-evidence;
- a rule, standard or measurement framework;
- a live or scaled implementation with a concrete mechanism; or
- a strategic platform shift with a concrete mechanism.

The shortlist is machine triage, not a publishing recommendation. Before an item becomes part of the reviewed Inbox or a post, the human review still records:

- mechanism;
- evidence source;
- outcome;
- caveat;
- relevance, evidence, novelty and learning value scores.

Only reviewed items scoring at least 14/20 reach the Inbox. No post is drafted or published automatically.

## Changes made after the audit

- Replaced the broad keyword score with implementation, outcome, mechanism, failure, standards and platform-shift signals.
- Added freshness penalties and penalties for generic event recaps, partnership announcements and corporate news.
- Stopped treating the word “target” as Target Corporation and limited company matching to the headline and article opening.
- Removed “campaign” as sufficient evidence of a connected consumer journey.
- Expanded the taxonomy to martech operations, customer data and identity, adtech, commerce, connected journeys, measurement, AI governance and regulation.
- Added a five-item Leadership Signals view to the dashboard.
- Added primary sources for Adobe Experience Cloud, Meta ads engineering, Google Ads and Commerce, Amazon Ads and IAB Tech Lab.
- Kept TikTok for Business visible as Pending access after repeated server timeouts.
- Increased durable excerpts from 24 to 120 words so prior articles can be re-ranked more accurately without storing full bodies in Git.
- Added automated tests for false-positive campaigns, implementation cases, standards, failures, partnerships and official newsroom indexes.

## Editorial direction for future publishing

The eventual public output should not repeat the article. A strong Aayush perspective should add three layers:

1. **The mechanism:** what data, technology and operating process actually connect.
2. **The implication:** what changes for a CMO, digital leader, commerce leader or martech owner.
3. **The boundary:** what the evidence does not prove, what could fail and what a leader should verify before copying it.

That is the difference between sharing news and developing a durable point of view.
