# Martech Radar

**Purpose:** Build durable judgment in MarTech, consumer digital and digital commerce. The daily output is a short reading decision, not a news dump or an automatic LinkedIn post.

## Editorial lens

Prioritize ideas that help explain or lead work like your Coca-Cola customer-data, consent, clean-room and agentic-marketing projects; your Samsung lifecycle and CRM growth engine; and senior MarTech, consumer digital, commerce and transformation roles. Favor the connection between technology choices and consumer or commercial outcomes.

The role lens comes from job descriptions and searches already discussed in other chats. These are examples of the responsibilities you are aiming for, not claims that the openings remain live:

| Role example | Capability the radar should help you discuss |
| --- | --- |
| Reckitt IT&D Senior Manager, E-commerce; PepsiCo Digital Commerce | Omnichannel commerce, retail media, digital shelf, platform strategy and attribution. |
| ETML by McKinsey Senior Manager, MarTech & Hyper-Personalization; Accenture Data Insights & Audiences Senior Manager | CDP, identity, Adobe AEP/AJO, audience strategy, next-best action, transformation roadmaps and executive advice. |
| IHCL CRM leadership; Lenskart lifecycle growth | Loyalty, large-scale CRM, segmentation, journey orchestration, channel coordination and lifetime value. |
| Whirlpool D2C & MarTech; Albertsons Marketing/Loyalty/Personalization product roles | Consumer platforms, product roadmaps, AI decisioning, experimentation, privacy and cross-functional delivery. |
| ADP Marketing & Advertising Technology | Data and platform integration, automation, APIs, vendor governance and reliable operations. |

Use these roles to tune research priority. This radar is a knowledge and ideas system; the separate job watches remain the place to track vacancies.

Core capabilities:

1. Customer data, identity, CDPs, first-party data, privacy and consent.
2. Lifecycle growth, CRM, loyalty, personalization and experimentation.
3. Digital commerce, retail media, conversion and measurement.
4. Applied AI and agentic commerce, with evidence of actual use.
5. Operating models, implementation lessons and regulation affecting the above.

## Three standing beats — check each daily

Every run checks all three beats and shows one short status line for each in the brief. A beat with no verified, worthwhile development says **No qualifying new item**. The best 1–3 stories across the beats become the reading priorities. A story may qualify for more than one beat, but appears only once. Across each week, aim to surface at least one strong item from each beat when the evidence supports it.

### 1. Major FMCG / CPG companies

Ask what large consumer companies are actually building, changing or measuring in marketing and commerce technology. Track Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, P&G, Unilever, Reckitt, Nestlé, Mondelez, L'Oréal, Colgate-Palmolive, Mars, Danone, Haleon and relevant Indian groups such as Tata Consumer, ITC, Godrej Consumer and Marico. Include retail and quick-commerce partnerships when they change the consumer journey. Look beyond campaigns to content systems, customer-data foundations, retail media, digital shelf, loyalty, consent, AI and operating models.

### 2. Fortune-scale enterprise technology

Look at how a major enterprise outside the FMCG beat deploys customer technology at scale: retailers, marketplaces, travel/hospitality, financial services, telecom, media and consumer electronics. Examples to scan include Walmart, Target, Amazon, Albertsons, Nike, Starbucks, McDonald's, Disney, Marriott, Hilton, American Express and Samsung. Focus on the business problem, the architecture or operating change, adoption and measurable effect. If calling a company **Fortune 500** or **Fortune 1000**, verify membership for the relevant year; otherwise say **large enterprise**.

### 3. A connected campaign or customer journey

Find a campaign where technology links at least two moments in the consumer journey, such as packaging or store to app, media to commerce, purchase to CRM, loyalty to personalization, or offline activity to measurement. For a strong case, reconstruct **consumer insight → trigger/data signal → decisioning → channels/touchpoints → consumer action → measured outcome**. Prefer cases with a clear mechanism, disclosed metrics and an identifiable brand. A creative award, QR code or multiple channels alone does not establish a connected journey.

Search brand and agency case studies, Effie and other effectiveness archives, platform customer stories and independent reporting. Check whether the case is freely readable before making it a top reading recommendation. Label vendor or agency claims and separate engagement, attributed sales and incremental lift.

## Secondary watch

Keep scanning tools and platform changes, privacy or advertising regulation, retail media, measurement methods, consumer behavior shifts and useful Reddit practitioner signals. Elevate one when it changes a decision in the three standing beats or introduces a genuinely important new capability. Include failures, implementation friction or counterexamples when verified; they often teach more than a launch announcement. Note especially India and South Asia implications alongside global lessons.

Once every four weeks, compare a small set of relevant senior job descriptions with the role lens above. Capture repeated or newly requested capabilities as a **skills-demand signal**, without turning this radar into another vacancies feed.

## Daily scan at 8:00 AM India time

Search the open web, not only a fixed feed. Scan company newsrooms, investor presentations, annual reports and earnings materials; first-party release notes and case studies from Shopify, Google, Meta, Adobe, Braze, Salesforce and other relevant platforms; and independent reporting such as MarTech, AdExchanger, Marketing Dive and Retail Dive. Use campaign and effectiveness archives for the connected-journey beat. Check relevant Reddit discussions (for example r/PPC, r/ecommerce and r/marketingautomation) for practitioner problems and early signals. Check official regulators for India, the EU, the UK and the US when a legal item appears. Expand sources when a strong lead points elsewhere. Rotate the company watchlists so every named priority gets a deliberate check across each week.

Scan new announcements from the past 7 days, substantial enterprise reports from the past 30 days, campaign case studies from the past 180 days, and regulatory changes from the past 30 days. Campaigns often disclose their mechanics and results months after launch; record both the campaign period and case-study publication date when available. An older item can enter only if it is unusually instructive and clearly marked **evergreen**. Use the original publication or effective date; a refreshed search listing is not a new event.

Open each candidate's source page. Prefer an original announcement, primary case study, research paper or regulator document. Record who makes each claim. A Reddit post is a signal to investigate, never sole evidence for a factual or legal conclusion. For case studies, distinguish conversion or engagement metrics from incremental revenue and note when the evidence is vendor supplied. For rules, distinguish proposal, enactment and effective date; link to the official text.

## Selection and ranking

Every candidate must pass four gates: relevant to the editorial lens; verified from an opened page; a genuinely new story or a material update; and enough substance to teach a decision or mechanism. Reject generic AI hype, unattributed statistics, thin press releases, unreadable paywalled cases, and repeat coverage of the same announcement. A company partnership without an implementation detail or observable change is a low-priority watch item, not a lead story.

Score passing candidates from 0 to 5 on each dimension: **role relevance**, **evidence quality**, **novel insight**, and **practical learning value**. Deliver the top 1–3 items with scores of at least 14/20. Scores rank reading priority; they are not scientific measurements. A day with no qualifying item is a valid outcome. Keep a source mix over each week and across the three beats; do not fill a daily quota with weak items.

For each selected item, give: the original link and date; beat; a two-sentence summary; why it matters for your work; the technology or journey mechanism; the outcome and how it was measured; one question to think about while reading; evidence caveat; estimated reading time; and a possible future LinkedIn or website angle. A content angle is an idea for your consideration, not a claim ready to publish.

## Repeat control and archive

The machine-readable `work/radar-seen.jsonl` log records each evaluated story's canonical URL, stable story key (company + product or event + material change), first-seen date and disposition. Before selecting, compare both URL and story key with the entire log and the recent briefs. A follow-up story is eligible only if it changes the underlying decision; label it **Update to [prior item]**. Save a dated brief under `outputs/briefs/` after every run with a status for all three standing beats, including **No qualifying new item** where appropriate. Notify in chat only when there is a worthwhile new reading recommendation. Maintain the [Reading inbox](Martech%20Radar%20%E2%80%94%20Reading%20Inbox.md) with the three current priorities and status (`To read`, `Read`, `Saved`, `Skip`). Never overwrite a past brief.

## How this becomes thought leadership

For the first two weeks, read or skim only the top item each day and note one observation from your own work. Once a week, review what recurred, what proved weak, and what you disagree with. Only then turn one insight into a minimal LinkedIn post. A deeper explainer can follow when a theme has enough evidence and your own point of view. Start the newsletter after there is a repeatable body of work, rather than making the newsletter another daily obligation.
